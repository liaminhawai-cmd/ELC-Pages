🧬 Unit 1 · Genetics

A gene is a short section of DNA, stored on a chromosome inside the nucleus of a cell. You get one copy of most genes from each parent. Different versions of the same gene are called alleles. The pair you carry is your genotype; what you actually show is your phenotype. A Punnett square is a table for listing every possible combination of alleles from two parents, so you can find the probability of each outcome.

🦠 Unit 2 · Pandemic — data literacy

A pandemic has spread across many countries. An epidemic is a fast rise in one area. An endemic disease is always there, at a steady level. Behind every headline there is a claim, and behind the claim there should be evidence. Your job is to check the maths. Most misleading claims break one of two rules: how to combine a percentage, and the difference between a steady rate and exponential growth.

📓 Pandemic words

🧮 Two traps in the news

Trap 1 — protection does not add up

An article says:

“Masks stop 50% of infections. The vaccine stops 60%. Together that is 110% protection.”

Nothing can be more than 100% protected, so this claim is misleading. Here is the correct working. Do not think about the protection — think about the risk that is left.

  1. 50% protection leaves 0.5 of the risk.
  2. 60% protection leaves 0.4 of that risk.
  3. 0.5 × 0.4 = 0.2, so 20% of the risk is left.
  4. 100% − 20% = 80% is stopped.

Rule: multiply what is left, then take it off 100%. Never add two percentages of protection.

Trap 2 — steady growth is not the same as doubling

Two towns report new infections. Both start with 10 cases on day 1.

TownDay 1Day 2Day 3Day 4Day 10
A: + 10 a day10203040100
B: × 2 a day102040805120

Town A grows in a linear way — it adds the same number every day. Town B grows in an exponential way — it multiplies by the same number every day. On day 1 and day 2 the two towns look the same. By day 10, Town A has 100 cases and Town B has 5120 — about 51 times as many.

Rule: check whether the numbers go up by + the same or by × the same. A doubling trend is not a steady rate.
