# First contact: where the sources come from

All three sources are public domain. Each was read in the copy named below.
Every word shown on the wall and the worksheet is the source's own: a […]
marks words left out. The plain English versions are written for this
resource and are labelled as such.

## Source A: James Cook, 1770

- Cook's journal of the Endeavour voyage, National Library of Australia, MS 1,
  page 299. The passage begins his general description of New South Wales,
  written after the daily entry for 23 August 1770 (Torres Strait), one day
  after he took possession of the east coast at Possession Island.
- Read in the South Seas transcription by Paul Turnbull:
  https://paulturnbull.org/project/southseas/journals/cook_remarks/092.html
- Cook's spelling is kept. The transcription's insertion marks (^) are
  removed. Cut: the sentence about climate and clothing.

## Source B: William Dampier, 1688 (published 1697)

- A New Voyage Round the World (London, 1697), as reprinted in R. H. Major,
  Early Voyages to Terra Australis (Hakluyt Society, 1859), via Wikisource:
  https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Early_Voyages_to_Terra_Australis/Account_of_the_Observations_of_Captain_William_Dampier
- The 1859 reprint modernises capitals. Dampier anchored on 5 January 1688.
- Cut on purpose: the sentence comparing the people with the "Hodmadods"
  (a derogatory name for the Khoikhoi of southern Africa), and the next
  paragraph's physical descriptions and racial slurs. "They differ but little
  from brutes" is kept: it is the perspective the lesson asks students to
  name and judge.

## Source C: Mahroot, 1845

- Evidence of Mahroot ("alias the Boatswain"), Monday 8 September 1845,
  Report from the Select Committee on the Condition of the Aborigines, NSW
  Legislative Council, ordered to be printed 31 October 1845.
- Read in the transcription by Hunter Living Histories, University of
  Newcastle: https://hunterlivinghistories.com/2018/01/04/1845-aborigines
  (scan of the original report on the same page).
- Four exchanges, in the order they were asked; others are left out. The Q
  and A labels are added. "Black fellows" is the committee's wording.
- To do before wider use: check the four exchanges against the scan, not
  only the transcription.

## Cultural safety

Mahroot is a named Aboriginal man who has died. The colonisation hub page
carries the advice to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander readers. Source B
is racist on purpose: it is on the sheet as evidence of a perspective, and
the lesson page tells the teacher to name it as racist when it is read.
