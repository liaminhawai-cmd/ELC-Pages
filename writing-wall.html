📝 Analytical Writing Wall

The same idea, written at every band. See what actually changes between C2 and VCE — then try lifting your own paragraph a level.

The rubric

Stays the same no matter which text type you're writing about.

Skill area C2 · Describe C3 · Explain C4 · Analyse VCE1 · Interpret
Ideas & Themes Recognises what happens in the text or scene. Retells key details with little interpretation. Explains basic feelings or ideas suggested by the text. Begins linking choices to simple themes. Connects specific language or film techniques to themes or concepts. Explains what the creator might be saying. Analyses how choices express messages, values, or perspectives. Analyses the creator's purpose in representing social or cultural issues.
Evidence & Metalanguage Gives a simple quote or description from the text or scene. Names a language or film feature and begins linking it to meaning. Uses terminology accurately with short, embedded evidence. Integrates evidence and metalanguage fluently to build interpretation.
Language & Structure Writes short, simple sentences using mostly literal verbs ("shows", "uses"). Expands sentences with connectives such as because, so, or to. Uses basic evaluative words. Uses more complex sentences and analytical verbs ("suggests", "highlights"). Writes fluently in a cohesive formal style, varying sentence structures to show cause, effect and interpretation.
Purpose & Interpretation Identifies the basic meaning or message. Explains the effect on the reader or viewer. Makes inferences about the creator's intent or perspective. Discusses deeper social, moral or ethical ideas, linking choices to broader context.
Skill focus across the bandsSay what happens → Explain effect or meaning → Link language choices to ideas or themes → Link literary, structural and stylistic features to messages, values and creator's intent
C2 Describe

"The author uses repetition to show that the tree keeps giving when it says, 'And the tree was happy.'"

Simple subject + verb + object. Names a language feature and states what happens in the text. Meaning stays close to the words on the page.

C3 Explain

"The repetition of the phrase 'the tree was happy' makes the tree seem generous, encouraging the reader to feel sympathy for her."

Adds an effect phrase to explain how the language influences the reader. Uses evaluative words and basic cause–effect reasoning.

C4 Analyse

"Silverstein uses repetition of the line 'And the tree was happy' to highlight the one-sided relationship, suggesting themes of sacrifice and imbalance."

Links language choice, effect and theme in one sentence. Uses analytical verbs ("highlight", "suggest") to move beyond description.

VCE1 Interpret

"By repeatedly portraying the tree as endlessly giving through the recurring motif 'And the tree was happy,' Silverstein invites readers to question cultural expectations around self-sacrifice in unequal relationships."

Combines language choice, ideas and values. Analyses the author's message and connects it to broader historical, social and cultural ideas.

C2 Describe

"The writer states that schools should not tell children what to eat when they argue that 'schools should not control students' diets'."

Simple subject + verb + object. Identifies the writer's opinion and states what is said. Verbs are literal ("states", "says") and ideas are concrete.

C3 Explain

"This statement encourages readers to agree because the claim 'schools should not control students' diets' sounds confident and reasonable."

Adds an effect or reason clause to explain how the statement sounds to the reader. Begins using evaluative adjectives ("reasonable", "strong") with connectives like because or so.

C4 Analyse

"By presenting the claim 'schools should not control students' diets' as an assertion, the writer reinforces the idea that parents should control children's choices."

Links language choice, effect and idea in one longer sentence. Uses analytical verbs ("reinforces", "frames") and abstract nouns like control, responsibility, choice.

VCE1 Interpret

"By framing parental responsibility as 'common sense', the writer promotes individual choice over institutional control, reflecting values of personal freedom."

Combines several ideas smoothly. Uses metalanguage to comment on the writer's perspective, connecting language choices to purpose, values or beliefs.

Skill focus across the bandsSay what happens → Add meaning → Link to ideas or themes → Discuss message and director's intent
C2 Describe

"The film uses a long shot to show the girls walking alone."

Simple subject + verb + object. Names the film technique and tells what is happening on screen. Verbs are literal ("shows", "uses").

C3 Explain

"The long shot makes the audience see how far the girls must travel, showing their bravery."

Adds a purpose phrase using "to + verb" to show effect on the audience. Introduces descriptive/feeling words ("brave", "alone") with connectives like because or so.

C4 Analyse

"Noyce uses a long shot to highlight the girls' isolation in the vast desert, symbolising their determination and loss of freedom."

Links technique, purpose and theme in one longer sentence. Uses analytical verbs ("highlight", "symbolise") to connect the image to a bigger idea.

VCE1 Interpret

"Noyce repeatedly uses wide landscape shots to contrast the beauty of the land with the cruelty of the government's control, emphasising the theme of resilience against oppression."

Combines several ideas smoothly. Uses metalanguage to comment on the director's choices, connecting film language, tone and theme in one flowing idea.
