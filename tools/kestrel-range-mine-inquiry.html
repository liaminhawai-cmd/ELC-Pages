What the Traditional Owners say

For tens of thousands of years the Traditional Owners have lived on and cared for this Country.

Two places here matter deeply to them. The rock shelter on the northern ridge holds very old paintings and is a place of learning and ceremony. The waterhole below it is a living water place that has always been looked after.

The Elders ask that these places, and the ground around them, are not dug up or damaged, and that the community is part of any decision about the land. These places are also protected by law as cultural heritage.

Tourism in Brackton

Visitors come to the Kestrel Range for two things: the view from the gorge lookout in the east, and the walk to the waterhole. They drive in along the scenic road.

Tourism brings money and jobs to the town all year round. A mine that can be seen from the lookout, or that blocks the road or the waterhole walk, would turn visitors away.

The weather here

The Kestrel Range is dry country. It gets very little rain, about 280 mm a year, less than half of what Melbourne gets.

When plants are cleared in a dry place like this, the bare ground dries out and blows away, and new plants struggle to grow back. Over time the land can turn to desert. This is called desertification.
